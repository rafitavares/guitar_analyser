      How to use the simulator

      The simulator models a complete railway traction converter — from the supply (catenary, third rail, generator, battery or fuel cell) through the DC link to the traction motors and the train. Everything runs inside this page; nothing is sent anywhere.

      1. Choose a System and Supply in the side panel.
      2. Press ▶ START and watch the start-up sequence.
      3. When the status shows READY, the train accelerates to the target speed.
      4. Press ■ STOP to brake and switch the system off.

      1 · Configuration (side panel)

      System
      Type of energy supply: DC overhead line, third rail DC, AC overhead line, diesel-electric, battery, hydrogen fuel cell, or auxiliary converter only.
      Supply (EN 50163)
      Nominal line voltage. The countries that use it are shown below the selector.
      Vehicle
      Metro, EMU, locomotive or regional unit. Sets mass, power, motors and maximum speed.
      Motor converter
      2-level or 3-level NPC inverter.
      Optional modules
      Tick or untick HF (harmonic filter), VLU (brake chopper), HBU (auxiliary converter), HWR (cooling inverter) and ESC (energy storage) to build your own configuration. The diagram, the sequence and the calculations follow your choice.

      Configuration can only be changed with the train at standstill.

      2 · Buttons and operating modes

      ▶ START
      Runs the automatic start-up sequence step by step (shown in Start-up sequence with a progress bar per step).
      ■ STOP
      Brakes the train to a stop, then opens contactors and breakers and lowers the collector.
      ⚠ EMERGENCY
      Opens everything immediately and applies the emergency brake.
      ↺ RESET
      Clears faults. Only possible with the train stopped.
      Automatic
      The sequence and the speed control are done for you.
      Manual
      You operate the equipment yourself by clicking the diagram: pantograph / collector shoes, breaker (HSCB/VCB), ChCt, CtL, line converter, ESC, fuel cell, diesel engine, HBU, HWR and motor converter. Wrong sequences have real consequences — for example, closing CtL without pre-charge trips the breaker.

      3 · Driving and line conditions

      Target speed
      (Automatic) speed the train accelerates to and holds.
      Traction / brake
      (Manual) master controller from −100 % (brake) to +100 % (traction). Keyboard ↑ / ↓ also work.
      Direct V/f (test)
      (Manual) set the motor frequency and voltage yourself to see slip, torque and current limits.
      Time scale
      Speeds up the simulation (1× to 10×).
      Switching frequency
      Scales the IGBT switching frequency (50–160 %).
      Line voltage
      No-load voltage of the supply, with the EN 50163 limits marked. Low voltage reduces the available power.
      Line receptivity
      (DC lines) how much regenerated braking energy the line can take. At 0 % the energy goes to the VLU.
      Gradient, ambient temperature, rail condition
      Affect running resistance, HVAC load and adhesion.

      4 · Electrical diagram

      • Line colours: orange supply, blue DC link, yellow AC output, green energy storage. Grey means de-energized.
      • Moving dashes show the direction of power flow; the speed of the dashes follows the power.
      • Boxes light up when a converter is running; the VLU turns red when it dissipates energy.
      • The bar next to the DC link capacitor shows its charge; the battery symbol shows the state of charge.
      • Values next to each module show voltages, currents, powers and temperatures.

      5 · Indicators

      • Gauges: train speed, motor frequency, DC link voltage (red zone = VLU / overvoltage) and motor converter power (orange = traction, green = regeneration).
      • Tiles: tractive effort and the limit that is active (max effort, power, adhesion, breakdown, current), converter current, pulse pattern, THD, motor power factor and efficiency, temperatures, line or battery data, auxiliaries and VLU.
      • Power flow: bars pointing right feed the DC link, bars pointing left take power out of it.
      • Event log: every switching action, warning and fault with its time.

      6 · Charts

      DC link & supply
      Last 60 s of DC link voltage, supply voltage, supply current and motor converter current.
      Motor converter PWM — phase U
      How the switching is generated: reference signal compared with the carrier(s). The note shows the pulse pattern in use (asynchronous, synchronous N-pulse or block).
      Motor modulation
      Two stacked charts. Top: Leg U–0 (blue) with the three motor currents: output of one inverter leg against the DC-link midpoint — 2-level: ±Vdc/2; 3-level NPC: +Vdc/2, 0, −Vdc/2. Bottom: Line U–V (yellow): voltage between two motor terminals, the difference of two legs — 2-level: 3 levels (+Vdc, 0, −Vdc); 3-level: 5 levels (±Vdc, ±Vdc/2, 0). In 3-level at low speed (m < 0.5) Line U–V only uses 0 and ±Vdc/2; the ±Vdc steps appear as the modulation index rises. More levels means a waveform closer to a sine and lower current ripple.
      Harmonic spectrum U–V
      Frequency content of the line-to-line voltage, with voltage and current THD.
      Train dynamics
      Last 120 s of speed, electric effort, mechanical brake and motor converter power.
      DC link & line side — 100 ms
      High-resolution view of the DC link: ripple, line filter oscillations, and the AC line voltage and current on AC systems.

      Drag horizontally on a chart to zoom; double-click to reset. Click a legend item to hide or show a curve.

      7 · System data tab

      • System configuration and Converter modules: ratings of every part of the selected system.
      • Tractive and braking effort vs speed: maximum effort curves calculated from the model, with the current operating point.
      • Supply voltages — EN 50163: voltage limits of all European systems; the selected one is highlighted.
      • Energy meters: energy taken from the supply, regenerated, dissipated, used by auxiliaries, battery, hydrogen or diesel consumption, and kWh/km.

      8 · Tips

      • A red FAULT status and a banner on the train view explain what tripped. Stop the train and press RESET.
      • To see regeneration go to the VLU, set Line receptivity to 0 % and brake.
      • With ESC fitted, switch to Manual and lower the pantograph while running: the battery keeps the train going.
      • Compare 2-level and 3-level in the Motor modulation and Harmonic spectrum charts.
      • The file works offline and can be shared as a single HTML file.
